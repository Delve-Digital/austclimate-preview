<p>Also known as ceiling cassette units, they are a subtype of AC for smaller spaces. Let’s look at some of the reasons to install one:</p>
<h4><strong>They’re compact</strong></h4>
<p></p>
<p>If you have limited wall or floor space, bulkhead AC options are compact and sleek for spaces like schools, offices, or hospitals.</p>
<p>Older air conditioners are bulky and intrusive, but bulkhead (despite its name!) is almost invisible once installed. Not only is it safer in the long scheme of things, but it also meets Australian building regulations.</p>
<h4><strong>They’re efficient</strong></h4>
<p></p>
<p>A single outdoor system supports the operation of up to four indoor units that are still energy efficient.</p>
<p>If you’re operating one bulkhead AC indoors, it is operated at a sensible limit for your home or business.</p>
<h4><strong>They run quietly</strong></h4>
<p></p>
<p>The fans and compressors are very quiet, so this is an awesome feature to have on the side of an air conditioning device.</p>
<p>Since there is no ductwork involved during installation as well, it’s a quieter option.</p>
<h4><strong>Independent control</strong></h4>
<p></p>30:T579,<p></p>
<p>Do you have a number of rooms to cool/heat, but each family member has their own preferences for comfort? A bulkhead AC means you can control each individual unit.</p><p>With adjustable zones, airflow is directed at specific areas of your home, so you can cool your workspace for remote work or heat your bedroom for sleep.</p>
<p>This feature isn’t found in ducted systems that run with a single unit; the process is similar for multi-head split systems where zoning is involved.</p>
<h5><strong>What are some reasons it isn’t right for your cooling needs?</strong></h5>
<p></p>
<ul>
<li>&gt; Unlike a wall-mounted or standard ducted system, a bulkhead AC needs to be placed in a specific way.</li>
<li>&gt; It’s not always suitable for your property. A bulkhead unit uses a big portion of the ceiling space, so it won’t work if there are building constraints (like studs).</li>
<li>&gt; Challenging maintenance and repairs to do yourself, as it's concealed in the ceiling cavity.</li>
</ul>

<p>A split system air conditioner is made up of two separate units: one inside your home and one outside. The indoor unit is responsible for blowing warm or cool air directly into the room, while the outdoor unit houses key components like the compressor and condenser.</p>
<p>For Melbourne homes, where you might just want to cool a living area or heat a bedroom during chilly mornings, a split system is a practical (and popular) solution.</p>
<h5><strong>How does a split system work?</strong></h5>
<p></p>
<p><strong>Indoor unit</strong>: Mounted to the wall inside the room, it contains the evaporator coil and a fan to circulate air.</p>
<p><strong>Outdoor unit</strong>: Installed outside, this unit includes the compressor, condenser coil, and expansion valve.</p>
<p><strong>Refrigerant cycle</strong>: A refrigerant moves between the two units, absorbing heat from indoors, releasing it outside, or reversing the process when heating.</p>
<p><strong>Smart temperature contro</strong>l: Most models come with a remote or app-based control, letting you adjust temperature, fan speed, and more.</p>
<p>There are also different types of split systems: wall-mounted, multi-split and floor-mounted.</p>

40:Tc04,<p>When looking at bulkhead systems and split systems, the difference lies in the design of the indoor unit.</p>
<p>A bulkhead air conditioner’s indoor unit is concealed within a wall or ceiling (usually a drop ceiling), making it much less visible. In contrast, a split system’s indoor unit is mounted directly on the wall, fully exposed. In short, the bulkhead is integrated into the building, while the split system isn’t. Here are some similarities!</p>
<ul>
<li>&gt; Their cooling capacity, such as using similar cooling mechanisms like the circulation of refrigerant to absorb heat from indoor air and expel it outside.</li>
<li>&gt; Both are designed with energy-efficient technologies such as energy-saving modes or programmable thermostats.</li>
<li>&gt; Can be customised in terms of their features and capacity to suit every room’s cooling/heating needs.</li>
</ul>
<p></p>
<h5><strong>What are the main differences between them?</strong></h5>
<p></p>
<ul>
<li>&gt; Bulkhead systems aren’t as flexible design-wise since they require a wall-mounted installation. Split systems, on the other hand, can be installed on ceilings or walls.</li>
<li>&gt; Cost-wise, bulkhead installation can be higher due to structural modifications to accommodate the unit. Split systems can often be retrofitted into existing spaces!</li>
<li>&gt; Maintenance-wise, a bulkhead AC is challenging because of its design. A split system is easier as components are separated between the indoor and outdoor units.</li>
</ul>
