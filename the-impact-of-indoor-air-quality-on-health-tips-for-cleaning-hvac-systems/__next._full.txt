29:T651,<p>Indoor air quality is a complex issue as its quality is influenced by an array of factors. The dynamics of indoor air quality are shaped by humidity levels, temperature, and most notably, the presence of pollutants. Each of these elements plays a role in the quality of the air we breathe indoors.</p>
<p>Humidity, for instance, is a double-edged sword. On one hand, a certain level of humidity is necessary for our comfort and health, preventing dry skin and respiratory discomfort. Excessive humidity can lead to growth of mould and dust mites, both of which are common indoor air pollutants.</p>
<p>Temperature, too, is a key player. While primarily a comfort factor, extremes of temperature can also contribute to poor indoor air quality. High temperatures can increase the concentration of volatile organic compounds (VOCs), while cold temperatures can lead to increased humidity and mould growth.</p>
<p>Cold air itself can be a health risk. The <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535281/table/fm-ch1.tab1/">WHO</a> recommends a minimum indoor temperature of 18°C, and extended temperatures below this <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535294/">increases the risk</a> of asthma attacks, worsening COPD and infection.</p>
<p>Risks are also posed by indoor air pollution. These invisible irritants range from mould, dust mites, pet dander, tobacco smoke, and VOCs emitted from household products. 3a:Tbc8,<p>We often associate the comfort of heating and cooling with pleasant temperatures and a cosy ambience, but there's much more to it— the quality of air we breathe. Air quality doesn't just enhance comfort; it has a direct bearing on our overall health.</p><p>The benefits of clean indoor air extend from our physical well-being to our mental productivity, making us realise why it's so essential to keep our indoor environment pristine.</p>
<p>One of the most significant benefits of good indoor air quality is improved respiratory health. Breathing clean air, devoid of pollutants like dust mites or mould spores, means our lungs aren't working overtime to filter out these harmful particles.</p><p>This can lead to fewer respiratory issues, reduced incidences of breathlessness, and even a decreased risk of developing chronic lung diseases.</p>
<p>Moreover, it's not just individuals with existing respiratory conditions that benefit. Even those without any known respiratory ailments can experience improved lung function. Clean air enables more efficient oxygen-carbon dioxide exchange in our lungs, leading to better physical health.</p>
