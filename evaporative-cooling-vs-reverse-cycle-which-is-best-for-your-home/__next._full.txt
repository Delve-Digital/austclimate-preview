27:T642,<p>According to <a href="https://www.sustainability.vic.gov.au/">Sustainability Victoria</a>, evaporative coolers draw in hot air through a series of wet filter pads, which are supplied with water from a tank at the base of the unit. Wall-mounted and ducted systems draw in hot air from outside, whereas portable units draw in room air.</p>
<p>As a cost-effective method of cooling your home or office, they can also keep indoor environments fresh and clean by filtering the air as it passes through the system.</p>
<p><strong>How does it work? </strong>By using the natural process of evaporation to lower air temperature, here is a quick breakdown:</p>
<ul>
<li>&gt; The unit draws in warm, dry air from the outside environment</li>
<li>&gt; As the air presses through the wet filter pads, water evaporates, absorbing heat from the air and cooling it</li>
<li>&gt; The cooled, moist air is distributed throughout the space through a network of vents and ducts</li>
</ul>
<p></p>
<p><strong>What are some of the key components of an evaporative cooling system? Let's take a look.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>&gt; <strong>The cooling unit</strong>. This is generally installed on the roof and houses wet filter pads and fan</li>
<li>&gt; <strong>Ductwork</strong>. These are a series of ducts that distribute cool air from the central unit to other rooms.</li>
<li>&gt; <strong>Vents</strong>. These are outlets in each room of a property that releases cool air into living spaces.</li>
<li>&gt; <strong>Water Supply</strong>, which keeps the filter pads moist for the evaporation process.</li>
</ul>
