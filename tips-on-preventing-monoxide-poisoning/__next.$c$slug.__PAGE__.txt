34:T574,<p><b>Carbon monoxide (CO) is colourless, odourless, and flavourless.</b> Known as the <i>"silent killer"</i>, this gas is produced as a by-product when you burn wood, charcoal, oil, propane, natural gas, and a range of other fuels.</p>
<p>If carbon monoxide is left to build up in semi-enclosed or enclosed spaces, it can enter your bloodstream and cause CO poisoning.</p>
<p><b>The common symptoms of CO poisoning include:</b></p>
<ul><li>Nausea</li><li>Vomiting</li><li>Dizziness</li><li>Headaches</li><li>General confusion</li><li>Chest Pain</li><li>Memory and coordination problems</li><li>Impaired judgement</li></ul>
<p></p>
<p>It's important to note that <i>low levels of carbon monoxide exposure can lead to poisoning with little to no symptoms.</i> Even with mild or invisible symptoms, <i>carbon monoxide exposure can lead to motor disorders, sensory disorders, miscarriage in pregnant women, permanent brain damage (including memory and learning impairments), personality and emotional changes, and more.</i></p>
<p>In the worst-case scenarios, <i>CO poisoning can lead to life-threatening heart damage, and ultimately, death. </i></p>
