28:T669,<p>Ductless split systems, also commonly referred to as mini-splits, eliminate the need for extensive ductwork and dramatically simplify the installation process.</p>
<p>There are two parts to any refrigerated air conditioner; the outdoor and indoor unit. In the outdoor unit, the refrigerant then enters the compressor as a low-pressure, superheated gas. The compressor sends the gas towards the condenser, where it is cooled and condensed, turning into a cool, high-pressure liquid.</p><p>This process produces hot air, which the condenser blows away with a fan. This is why, if you ever stand near an outdoor unit while it’s operating, you’ll feel a lot of hot air being blown out.</p>
<p>Returning inside, the cold refrigerant enters the evaporator, where two things happen. First, it absorbs heat from the space being cooled. This causes the refrigerant to boil, which turns it back into a superheated low-pressure gas. This process allows it to produce cool air, which the air conditioner’s indoor unit blows into the room.</p><p>Finally, the gas enters the compressor and the cycle starts over.</p>
<p>Despite the technical jargon, the concept is fairly straightforward: cool, compress, condense, evaporate, and distribute. It's this cycle that keeps your indoor environment comfortable, regardless of the scorching heat outside.</p>
<p>Ductless split systems differ from other air conditioning options through their unique design and functionality.
