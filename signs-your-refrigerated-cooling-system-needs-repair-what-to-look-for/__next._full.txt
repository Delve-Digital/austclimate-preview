25:T5b2,<p>All air conditioning systems use or produce water as a part of the cooling process. This is not normally an issue, but if a part of the system isn't working correctly, you may notice signs of a leak. These include damp patches on your wall or ceiling, mould growth and a musty, unpleasant smell.</p>
<p>Refrigerated air conditioning produces water as hot air cools in the evaporator. Usually, this water is collected in a drain pan and passes through the drain line into your plumbing system.</p><p>If the pan is damaged or the line becomes blocked or corroded, then the water can no longer drain effectively and may begin to pool. </p>
<p>These parts should be inspected and then cleaned or replaced by an air conditioning professional.</p>
<p>Refrigerant leaks can be more difficult to notice. As refrigerant is a gas at room temperature, you usually won't see it pooling or dripping. The most common signs of a refrigerant leak are bubbling or hissing noises, a reduction in cooling effectiveness, or longer-than-usual cycles.</p>
<p>There are a few causes of refrigerant leaks, and most can be avoided with a proper maintenance plan.</p><p>These include deteriorated seals and valves, broken or corroded pipes, or damage to pipes around the outdoor unit (usually by pets or children playing too close.) 32:T6cd,<p>If your once-quiet cooling system begins to make unusual sounds or starts to smell not quite right, it's important not to ignore it.</p>
<p>It is completely normal for your air conditioning to make some noises, namely, a few clicks and some fan noises during the start of a cycle, a gentle whooshing while it is running, and a few more clicks during the cool-down phase.</p><p>However, if your air conditioning is making unusual noises like banging, popping, rattling, hissing, screeching or buzzing, that could be a sign of a problem.</p>
<p>Some common causes include:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>Ductwork coming loose or breaking</li>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>Capacitor failure</li>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>Refrigerant leaks</li>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>The compressor fan is broken or obstructed</li>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>Loose components</li>
<li><strong>&gt; </strong>Wires or other electrical components are damaged</li>
</ul>
<p>While some of these are more minor issues, the best solution is to turn your system off and call a professional for a <a href="/austclimate-preview/cooling/refrigerated-cooling/">reverse cycle heating and cooling service</a>.</p>
<p>As for smells, a properly functioning air conditioner should have no discernable smell. If you notice a mouldy, musky or mildew smell, it is possible that there is a water leak in or near the evaporator.</p>
<p>A burning smell could be related to a buildup of dust in filters, or it could be a broken electrical component.
