A gas heater warms your spaces and keeps them comfortable during cold months, but it is essential for it to be running safely. Licensed gas fitters will inspect and test all aspects of your gas heater, and this includes:

- > They check for leaks and carbon monoxide emissions, so it’s not doing damage to your health

- > Test the safety controls, such as the thermostat and carbon monoxide detector

- > Check the pilot light and gas pressure gauge

- > Clean the inside of the heater and replace the air filters for ventilation and air flow

## 1. Your Heater Won’t Last As Long

Most manufacturers say that the lifespan for your gas heater is around 15 to 20 years. This is only true if the heater is maintained as per the manufacturer's recommendations. When you don’t service your gas heater, it is more likely to break down.

- > Not cleaning your heater doesn’t put a stop to debris/dust building up inside of it, which results in component failure or overheating

- > Moving parts that aren’t lubricated increase friction and unnecessary wear and tear

- > A burner that isn’t clean or fully functional puts more strain on the heating system

- > The thermostat or pressure valve won’t work properly

If you have any existing problems with your heating, such as holes in the vents, ignition failure, or broken circuitry, it won’t be repaired, so you’ll risk these problems getting worse. The last thing you want is to spend thousands of dollars on a replacement unit!

Depending on your warranty as well, many of these policies require an annual heater service to keep this active.

02
## 2. Your Heater Won’t Be Energy Efficient

A yearly service for your gas heater will pare back your energy consumption, such as using less gas to heat a room.

Sustainability Victoria states that the running costs for heating a medium (160 sqm) home vary from around $1,200 per year for multi-split reverse-cycle air conditioning all the way up to more than $2,700 for ducted gas heating.

Your energy expenses will be higher, and with worse energy efficiency, you’ll hike up your carbon footprint and environmental impact as well. You also won’t be able to offset maintenance costs since you won’t save on utility bills!

A lack of consistent maintenance strains your heater, so smaller issues are bound to become bigger problems later down the line.

![What Happens If You Don't Service Your Gas Heater? 7 Costly Consequences](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Delve-Digital/austclimate-preview/preview/media/fdbe9b88c681-Australian-Safety-Standards.webp)

From August 10, 2022, the installation of any new or replacement gas ducted heaters that do not comply with the updated Australian Standard AS5263.1.3 is not permitted in Victoria. For example, it is illegal to sell, supply or install new or second-hand open-flued gas space heaters that do not meet new and improved safety specifications.

Some ducted heaters are open-flued and can be affected by negative pressure if there is insufficient ventilation.

This applies to ducted heaters when the appliance is installed indoors, and if this pressure affects the ducted heater, the air ducts around the house that push out hot air are not affected by the operation of exhaust fans.

If your gas heater does not comply with current Australian safety standards, your home will not be safe. Older heaters with open-flued technology, for example, may not meet the latest safety requirements in Victoria for both public residences and public housing to protect all properties.

Regular servicing is non-negotiable. According to [Energy Safe Victoria](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/), your gas heater should be serviced at least every two years by a qualified gasfitter, and a carbon dioxide test should be conducted at the heating vent outlets in all rooms. Some heaters need fresh air flow to operate safely. This way, you’ll lower your chances of dangerous incidents and keep your spaces on board with legal requirements.

03
## 4. There Will Be a Carbon Monoxide Safety Risk

Gas ducted heating can produce carbon monoxide as a by-product, so a leak from a lack of servicing is dangerous to your health and is potentially fatal. Poisoning symptoms include:

- > Dizziness, such as feeling unsteady or lightheaded

- > A dull and persistent headache

- > Nausea and stomach upset

- > Difficulty breathing

- > Experiencing mental fog or struggling to think clearly

- > Blurred or impaired vision

- > Pain or tightness in the chest

A regular service detects and repairs potential leaks, and this is especially true for older gas heaters.

No matter how confident you are, never DIY a gas heater service. Only a qualified professional can carry out this assessment and guarantee that your heater will comply with current safety standards.
