Split systems](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Delve-Digital/austclimate-preview/preview/ai/pages/heating/split-systems.md) have two main units: an indoor unit (evaporator) and an outdoor unit (condenser). Components of a split system include the compressor, evaporator coil, condenser coil, and expansion valve, which remove heat from indoor air and push it outside.

However, not all split systems are reverse cycle; some only cool the air inside a building, so you need to double-check with your supplier. A reverse-cycle split system has indoor and outdoor components, but they reverse the refrigeration cycle, heating as well as cooling.

09
## Why might a multi-head split system not be for you?

In terms of design complexity, multi-head options have more complex components to manage refrigerant levels of more than one indoor unit.

With longer pipe runs, indoor units may be located further away from outdoor units, increasing the installation and material price. They also must be installed by certified professionals, which can be more expensive.

But if you have multiple rooms (such as a double-storey property or office building) multi-head split systems will give you temperature flexibility and heat your spaces.

10
## Why might a split system be a better option?

A split system has a lower initial cost, an easier installation, and is cost-effective for single-room use.

Additionally, having only one indoor and outdoor unit means repair and maintenance aren’t going to be as pricey. That said, there’s potential for higher running costs if you’re using a split system to heat a large area.
