## How Does Gas Ducted Heating Work?

Before we dive too deep into the pros and cons, let’s cover the basics first. Gas ducted heating involves a central unit that is connected to a series of ducts and outlets. These ducts are tucked away out of sight (either above or below) and placed in strategic locations across your property.

Once the system is turned on, the air is drawn from indoors and transferred to the central heater. It is then warmed according to the temperature on the thermostat and distributed through the ducts via a fan. The final stage involves a gentle supply of air that flows through the vents connected to the ductwork.

After the desired temperature is reached, the unit will slowly come to a halt and ensure that the remaining heat is trapped inside. Unlike a reverse cycle system, gas ducted heating does not require the installation of an outdoor unit.

## Pro: Fast and Reliable

Across the board, gas ducted heaters are remarkably fast and reliable. Even in the depths of winter, they consistently deliver comforting whole-house warmth. A quality ducted system can warm up a large home or business almost instantly. 

This speed represents a stark contrast to [hydronic heating](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Delve-Digital/austclimate-preview/preview/ai/pages/heating/hydronic-heating.md), which may take approximately 10-15 minutes or more to make a noticeable effect on room temperature.

## Con: A Lack of Cooling

This is one of the obvious differences between gas ducted heaters and reverse cycle systems. Generally, gas ducted units heat while reverse cycle systems both heat and cool. Of course, we should note that there are options for add-on cooling.

## Con: Installation may be Challenging

Ducted heating won’t be suitable for everyone. Some properties will have spatial limitations that make installation inherently challenging and/or impractical. In these situations, it is important to remember that there are still many other great heating options available, including split systems, gas log fires, and hydronic heating.

## Pro: Adaptive Zoning Technology

Zoning is one of the major advantages of a modern ducted system. This feature allows you to have multiple individually controlled zones with their very own temperature and time settings.

Under this arrangement, you can set a completely different temperature in the living room and the bedroom – which is a great way to settle any arguments or disputes related to the thermostat setting.

Featuring as many as 10 individual zones that are easily controlled with a touchscreen, MyAir Zoning is a system we highly recommend. Thanks to intuitive smart device connectivity, you can control each zone from any location. As far as air conditioning management systems go, MyAir is in a league of its own. 

![Pros and Cons of Gas Ducted Heating](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Delve-Digital/austclimate-preview/preview/media/09c7a362e3ba-Pros-and-cons-of-gas-ducted-heating-energy-costs.webp)

With household bills and cost-of-living both on the rise, we understand that affordability is a major concern for many Melbournians. While gas systems with a lower star rating may be more affordable at first, they also have higher average running costs. This is why we recommend investing in an efficient unit.

As you can see in this graph from [Sustainability Victoria](https://www.sustainability.vic.gov.au/energy-efficiency-and-reducing-emissions/save-energy-in-the-home/heat-your-home-efficiently/calculate-heating-costs), a 6-star ducted system can heat the same area for roughly $700 less than a 3-star system.

If you want to lower your running costs, responsible temperature control is key. Energy Safe Victoria recommends that you set the temperature between 18 to 20 degrees at all times.

While this may seem like low numbers in conventional terms, the effectiveness of gas heating means that you very rarely need to set it any higher. 

On average, every degree higher than 20 results in a 10 per cent increase in energy bills.
